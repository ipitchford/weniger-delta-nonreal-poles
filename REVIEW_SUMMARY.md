# Review summary (internal, AI-assisted)

This is a public summary of the checks applied before release. It keeps the substantive objections and records how each was resolved. It is **not** peer review. Every reviewer was an AI system working inside the Evidence Press workflow. None of this is independent reproduction by an unaffiliated party, and none of it is formal verification. The original reports are kept privately. This summary contains no scores, ratings or recommendations.

## What was done

1. **Production checks (19 October 2026).** The 15-page first draft was produced with two exact verifiers and a bounded novelty search. A second agent in the same session checked the proofs and replayed the certificates.
2. **First round (23 October 2026).** The first draft received a multi-perspective AI-assisted review in five parts: an editor's report; three specialist perspectives (methodology and exact computation; sequence transformations and real-rooted polynomials; practical use and cross-disciplinary reach); and a devil's-advocate challenge to the core argument. All five read every proof. Between them they recomputed, in sympy and mpmath, every printed integer, the corner factorisations of the cubic discriminant, the constant (√33 − 5)/2, the tail-ratio bound, the degree-18 all-shift discriminant with its 19 negative coefficients and the Hankel minors, and replayed both verifiers byte for byte. No reviewer found a mathematical error. The domain reviewer read Borghi (2026) in full and found that the restatement of the question was faithful and that it is refuted as posed.
3. **Adversarial audit by a different model (23 October 2026).** An AI-assisted adversarial audit (configured as OpenAI gpt-6-astra) was instructed to refute, not confirm, and did not read the other reports. It wrote its own exact SymPy checks without importing package code: 48 general-order witnesses, 48 safe cubic quadruples, four shifts straddling the cubic threshold, 42 paired single-inequality isolation tests, the elimination-polynomial degrees and constants, and a symbolic resultant showing that the all-shift quartic has no cancellation at any positive shift. It found no critical or major defect and two minor points (below).
4. **Revision (23 October 2026).** The manuscript was retitled and rewritten (13 pages). It gained the persistence theorem for one fixed measure (Section 6), the positive theorem and natural families (Section 7), explicit certificates at k = 5–8 (Table 3), the n = 1 case of the all-shift quartic, the practical section (Section 8), a classification table, a status table, Figure 1 and 13 references. The two verifiers were merged into `code/verify_all.py`, which now also checks the cubic discriminant identity as a polynomial identity.
5. **Clean replay.** A separate copy of the revised package reproduced `results/verification.json` byte for byte under `python -B -O`.
6. **Verification re-review of the revised version (23 October 2026).** The revision was traced item by item against the twelve-item fix list and both audit items; every item was found addressed. The re-review replayed the verifier (byte-identical), rechecked every quoted fragment of Borghi (2026) against a freshly fetched copy of the published text, confirmed all 17 DOIs on Crossref, checked Tables 3 and 4 cell by cell against the certificate, and ran its own SymPy spot checks. It found no wrong theorem, and listed seven sentence-level corrections (C1–C7 below) and some optional polish.
7. **Second adversarial audit, of the revised version (23 October 2026).** The same external model, run in parallel with the re-review and without reading the other reports, attacked the new proofs (Theorems 6.1 and 6.3, Corollary 6.2, Lemma 7.1, Theorem 7.2, Corollary 7.3, Proposition 5.1 for n = 1) and then reviewed the edits made while it was working, including the restored all-shift corollary. It found no counterexample to any theorem. With its own SymPy code it reproduced the genuine-pole counts for k = 1–30, the Jensen counts to degree 12, the all-shift discriminants at n = 0 and 1 and the four real zeros at n = 2, all 24 tail-family cases, all 975 grid cases and 180 further cases, and replayed the verifier byte for byte. It raised one claim-scope point (R1) and three minor points (R2–R4, below). It also supplied two independent results: an exact rational Rouché certificate that the fixed measure's denominator has nonreal zeros at every k ≥ 204 (zeros only; it does not exclude cancellation, and it is not part of the paper), and a non-integer Jacobi example with genuine nonreal poles at β = 20.
8. **Final fix round.** The re-review's seven corrections and the audit's four points were made in a final editor pass, the audit's large-shift observation became Remark 7.4 with an exact certificate, and the verifier gained the corresponding checks. The final package (15 pages) was replayed byte for byte.

## Points the audit tested and found sound

The audit attacked the realisation lemma (log-convex cone versus actual measures, boundary indices, order of limits), the cubic endpoint, the bound R_k(b) < 4 for k ≥ 4, cancellation (accuracy order, Hankel indices, the elimination polynomial, feasibility of the one-moment perturbation), the all-shift quantifiers and the smoothing argument. Each survived. It recorded one stronger statement that is false and is not claimed: the all-shift measure has four real quartic roots at n = 2, β = 1.

## Remaining limitations

* All results are at fixed finite order; nothing is claimed about convergence of the delta approximants.
* Conjecture 6.4 (genuine nonreal poles at every k ≥ 3 for one measure) is open; k_0 in Theorem 6.3 is not effective.
* The all-shift quartic is certified for n = 0 and n = 1 only.
* The natural-family grid is finite; the K_ν and non-integer Jacobi cases are evidence, not proof.
* Hutchinson and Kurtz are cited without theorem locators.
* The material added at revision was checked by the verification re-review and the second adversarial audit, not by a second full multi-perspective round. No proof assistant was used.
* Two small presentation points from the second audit are not reflected in the text: the inequality 0 < h_j/h_4 in the proof of Corollary 5.2 should read 0 ≤ at the artificial endpoint b = 0 (the actual domain is b > 0), and "smallest K" in Table 3 is within the searched range K = 2^e, 1 ≤ e ≤ 16 (at K = 1 two k = 8, β = 5 cases already have nonreal zeros). Neither affects a result.
